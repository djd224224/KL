What's different today is that the wins were concentrated rather than scattered: three cities (Phoenix, San Antonio's B87.5, Louisville) each cleared their band by exactly 1°F, and together they accounted for $109.02 of the $184.40 in gross winnings. Philadelphia and Miami were the two clean losses that mattered. Philadelphia's 65°F landed in the dead center of its {64,65}°F band on 126 contracts — the single largest position of the day — for a −$43.08 hit before the B66.5 leg clawed back $12.24. Miami's 90°F did the same to its {89,90}°F band on 98 contracts for −$34.04. Both forecasts (64.5°F and 89.5°F respectively) were essentially exact, which is the structural problem this book keeps running into: forecast-centered bands are where the market prices NO richest and where the bot sizes biggest, and those are exactly the bands that lose when the forecast is right. What to watch: San Antonio's B87.5/B89.5 pair shows the fix already works when it's applied — spreading 170 contracts across two adjacent bands turned a single band's binary risk into a smoothed +$55.73. The same adjacent-band spread on Philadelphia (B64.5/B66.5) still lost net because the center band outweighed the edge band 102-to-24; sizing the two legs more evenly would have cut today's single biggest loss roughly in half.
