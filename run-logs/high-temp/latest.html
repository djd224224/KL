Ten in-band losses against five wins is the structural pattern repeating. The book sells NO on the bands the market prices richest — the ones straddling the forecast — so it wins only when the forecast misses and loses when it lands. Seattle shows it at scale. 225 contracts on {60,61}°F against a 62.0°F forecast that missed by only 1°F cool, the kind of near-miss that still lands in a hedge band when multiple adjacent bands are ordered (Seattle carried B60.5, B62.5, and B64.5). The two winners did the opposite: San Antonio's 90°F actual missed its 92.0°F forecast by a full 2°F, and the traded B92.5 band was far enough out to clear cleanly (+$46.97). Trenton's +$0.51 is immaterial in dollars but structurally the same story — a 1.5°F cool miss cleared the B75.5 band. Atlanta is the clearest illustration of the mechanism in one city: the 84°F actual matched the 84.0°F forecast exactly, so the band centered on it (B83.5) lost (−$36.82) while the band one notch below (B81.5) — ordered as a hedge, not a center bet — won (+$54.87). Net effect across both legs was a small gain, but only because the hedge happened to sit on the right side. What to watch: eight of the ten losses (all but Houston B90.5 and Dallas, which were small) are concentrated in exactly the cities where multiple adjacent NO bands were ordered around a forecast that turned out to be accurate — the overlapping-band hedge strategy pays for itself in frequency of small wins but produces outsized exposure, like Seattle's 225 contracts, exactly where the forecast is most trustworthy and least likely to miss.
