The pattern repeats what prior digests have flagged: fills concentrate on the band nearest the forecast, where the market prices YES richest, so the book wins only when the forecast misses and loses when it lands. Oklahoma City carried the heaviest single position of the day — 125.8 contracts on the 72–73°F band, filled between 3:04 AM and 2:47 PM, well ahead of and straddling the morning snapshot runs, and the 72°F high landed exactly inside it. Its 6.1-contract edge position one band lower caught the same print cleanly outside its own window, but at a fifth of the size it covered only 5% of the loss. Every offset came from a forecast miss that still cleared a band: Atlanta's 70°F high cleared the 68.5 band from below (+$67.87) even though its 70.5 band lost, and San Francisco's 65°F high cleared the 63.5 band from below (+$32.86) even though its 65.5 band lost — in both cases the bot's own ladder of bands turned one city into a split rather than a clean win or loss. Six ordered cities never filled at all, including Minneapolis, Newark and Washington DC's neighbors in temperature; of those, Dallas (77°F actual vs. a B77.5/B79.5 ladder) and New York City (62°F actual vs. a B62.5/B64.5 ladder) would also have landed in-band had they filled. What to watch: Oklahoma City's center-band sizing. A 125.8-contract position sized well ahead of the morning forecast runs, on a city with no size cap event in play, produced over a third of today's loss on its own. Confirming forecast runs before committing that much size to a single band — rather than letting overnight orders rest at full size into the morning read — is the lever available here.
