Overall Summary

July 8 was a full, healthy trading day — the bot is decisively back online after June's 11-day execution outage. It placed 60 markets across all 20 cities and got filled on 19 markets in 16 cities for 1,155 contracts and $578.85 of deployed capital, all on the NO side — the core strategy of fading well-forecast highs by selling the narrow straddle bands. On the 16 markets that have a final daytime CLI reading, the day estimates to +$270.67, a +52.7% return on the $513.71 resolved capital, with 11 winning bands against 5 losers. The engine fed it a clean setup: a hot, tightly-clustered day where forecasts were nearly exact — Dallas 100.0°F forecast → 101 actual, San Antonio 96 → 96, Houston 95 → 95, Miami 92.5 → 92, Atlanta 93 → 94 — which is precisely when NO-band selling pays, because the tails the model faded stayed empty. The biggest winners were the cities where actuals cleared the straddle: San Antonio +$58.13, Austin's B99.5 +$62.40, Atlanta and New Orleans +$47.70 each, Oklahoma City +$44.70, New York +$31.50. The five losses came from bands the market pinned exactly: Houston printed 95°F into a B95.5 short (−$15.60), Philadelphia 86°F into B85.5 (−$15.36), and Austin's B97.5 caught 98°F (−$61.50) — the day's single most expensive fill, offset within the same city by the winning B99.5. Three markets remain unresolved (Denver, Los Angeles, San Francisco, $65.14 capital) pending final west-coast CLI; Denver's IEM feed still shows only a stale midnight 73°F artifact, not the ~91°F daytime high the model forecast. Net: the strategy is trading again, calibrated, and profitable on the day.
